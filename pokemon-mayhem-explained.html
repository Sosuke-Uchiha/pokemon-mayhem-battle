Every match is a full knockout: both sides send Pokémon one at a time in the order you set (first card leads, last is your ace). Two Pokémon duel until one faints; the winner stays in — keeping its wounds — and faces the next one. When a whole team is down, the match ends.

2. Point costs & the 20-point budget

Each of the 1025 Pokémon has a hidden rating. Its point cost (1–10) is a curved function of that rating, so elite Pokémon are disproportionately expensive:

t = (rating − minRating) / (maxRating − minRating)
cost = max(1, round(1 + 9 × t2.2))

You get 20 points for up to 6 slots. Two maxed legendaries eat the whole budget — while ten points can buy five solid mid-tier fighters. Depth usually wins: a full bench of six means six duels' worth of fresh HP.

3. HP, fatigue & battle scars

Battle HP is normalized from the Pokémon's base HP stat:

maxHP = 70 + 30 × clamp((baseHP − 30) / 120, 0..1) → every Pokémon has 70–100 HP
1
Fatigue: a Pokémon's effective strength scales with its current HP — from 100% at full health down to 35% at the brink. Worn-down sweepers hit softer.
2
Battle scars: in the League, damage persists between rounds. Win the Quarterfinal 2-0 with a bleeding ace and it starts the Semifinal just as hurt.

4. Duels & turn order

Within a duel, the Pokémon with the higher Speed stat attacks first, then the two alternate. Speed ties are a coin flip. There are no misses — every attack lands for at least chip damage — so faster Pokémon effectively get a free extra hit.

5. The damage formula

damage = 24 × (attackerStr / defenderStr)0.6 × statRatio × typeMult0.8 × random(0.85–1.15) × crit
TermWhat it means
attackerStr / defenderStrEffective strength = (rating + boosts) × fatigue. Stronger, healthier Pokémon hit harder and tank better.
statRatioThe attacker's better offense (Atk or SpA) vs the matching defense (Def or SpD), clamped to ×0.85–×1.35. Big stat mismatches enable one-shots.
typeMultType effectiveness (0, ¼, ½, 1, 2, 4) softened by the 0.8 exponent.
crit8% chance, ×1.6 (Super Luck: 16%, Sniper: ×2.1, Shell/Battle Armor: never).

Then ability/passive multipliers apply (see below), and the result is clamped:

SituationMinMax
Normal hit565
Super-effective hit (×2 or ×4)5110 — a one-shot KO is possible
Blocked by an immunity ability—4 (chip only)
Because max HP is 70–100, only a super-effective hit (or a big crit) can KO a healthy Pokémon in one blow — which is exactly the hit Sturdy endures at 1 HP.

6. Type effectiveness

The full 18-type Pokémon chart is used. Each attacker "knows" one move per type it has (e.g. a Water/Ice Pokémon can throw Water or Ice moves), and effectiveness multiplies across the defender's two types — so ×4 and ×¼ hits exist. A true immunity (Ground vs Flying, Electric vs Ground…) reduces the move to guaranteed chip damage.

Team-wide type advantage also nudges close matches: before a match the engine compares how well each side's types line up and gives the better-covered team a small edge to strength.

7. Abilities

Every Pokémon has exactly one ability. About 120 iconic species carry their canonical signature (Charizard = Blaze, Gengar = Levitate, Azumarill = Huge Power…); everyone else gets one that fits its stat profile — walls lean defensive, attackers lean offensive. Assignment is deterministic, so a Pokémon's ability never changes between sessions.

8. Passives

A second, independent layer: every Pokémon also has one passive. ~60 canonical picks (Blaziken = Speed Boost, Scizor = Technician, Slowbro = Regenerator…), type-flavored status/heal passives for Electric/Fire/Poison/Ice/Water types, and stat-profile fallbacks for the rest.

Abilities and passives stack — Blaziken below ⅓ HP with a Fire move is +50% from Blaze, and can still Speed-Boost into a second strike.

9. Evolution, Mega & Gigantamax

1
Evolution — after winning a duel, a Pokémon with a stronger evolved form has a 30% chance to evolve on the spot. It gains the new form's stats and a +40 HP surge, and the evolution sticks for the rest of the League.
2
Mega Evolution / Gigantamax — when a Mega/G-Max-capable Pokémon is losing (weaker than its foe, or hurt below 55%), it has a 35% chance per duel to transform: +8 strength and +40 HP. Once per team per match.

10. The League bracket

16 teams, single elimination, 4 rounds: Qualifier → Quarterfinal → Semifinal → Final. The field is built so the drama escalates:

SlotWho
YouYour squad, in your chosen battle order.
6 boss trainersDrawn from a pool of 12 — the 8 Kanto gym leaders (rematch-era teams) plus champions Blue, Lance, Steven, Cynthia — with fixed canonical rosters, no budget cap.
9 rivalsJohto leaders (Falkner, Bugsy, Whitney, Morty, Chuck, Jasmine, Clair, Pryce, Janine) with random budget-20 teams of varying strength.

Your early opponents are drawn from the weaker random rivals; the boss trainers are seeded into the far side of the bracket, so you won't meet Cynthia before the Semifinal. Damage persists between rounds, and if you're knocked out the bracket plays on — the verdict tells you who took the trophy.

11. Draft mode

Six rounds of "pick 1 of 5": you and the CPU alternate first pick from a shared reveal. The CPU takes the highest-rated card 70% of the time (second-best otherwise). After six rounds each, the two squads fight one full match — no budget involved, pure eye for value.

12. How the AI plays

  • Move choice: 70% of the time the attacker picks its best type matchup; 30% it picks any non-immune move — so battles aren't perfectly deterministic.
  • Rival team building: budget rivals spread their points across 6 slots with per-slot randomness, so stronger rivals field deeper, pricier squads.
  • CPU battle order: opponents order their team for a strong lead and ace.
